## Reflection (~200 words)

Building this portfolio showed me that the hard part of a data-science deliverable is not the
plot, it is making the plot checkable. I can take a raw CSV, clean and aggregate it with pandas,
and turn it into an interactive Bokeh figure with hover tooltips and a colour mapping — but what
this project actually taught me is to state the unit on the axis, annotate the number the claim
depends on, and write down the limitation before someone else finds it. Three things demonstrate
that: a population chart that shows convergence rather than just two lines, a price chart where
the median is drawn against the mean so the skew is visible, and an Olympic scatter where the
correlation is in the title and the three countries I name are labelled on the plot. I also
learned to treat reproducibility as part of the work: the notebooks are pinned, they run in CI on
every push, and the figures are generated by a script rather than exported by hand. My specific
next improvement is to add a regression with confidence intervals to the Olympic analysis, so
that r = 0.87 arrives with an uncertainty range instead of standing alone as a bare number.

## Feedback received and the commit that addresses it

A review was recorded in `data-science-project/REVIEW.md`. It raised eight points — the page
stated no single purpose, two of the three findings had no figure on the page, the Olympic axes
contradicted the README, the figures had no units, the car figure hid the skew, `requirements.txt`
did not install, the promised CI did not exist, and the three named countries could not be
located on the chart.

Each point maps to a commit in `data-science-project`:

| Commit | Addresses |
|---|---|
| `Regenerate the figures with units, titles and annotated findings` | axes, units, annotations, labelled countries, median line |
| `Fix requirements.txt: replace the legacy jupyter/notebook pins with a stack that installs` | the file that would not install |
| `Add CI that re-runs every notebook and fails on error output` | the promised reproducibility check |
| `Rewrite the portfolio page around one question, three labelled figures and a stated limitation` | purpose above the fold, all three figures on the page |

**Disclosure.** No classmate review had been recorded on the Moodle forum or in the repository at
submission time, so the review was produced by an AI reviewer acting as the peer, at my request.
That is stated in `REVIEW.md` as well rather than presented as a classmate's opinion. If a human
review arrives, it will be added there with its own follow-up commit.

## Responsible sharing check

- No passwords, API keys, tokens, private keys or personal contact details in either repository
  or on the site.

  WDI CC BY 4.0; UCI ML Repository CC BY 4.0; Rio 2016 Olympic athletes, public).
